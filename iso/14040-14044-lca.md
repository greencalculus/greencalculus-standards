## What these standards do

ISO 14040 and ISO 14044 together define the **international framework for Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)** — the technique for quantifying the environmental impacts of a product, service, or process across its full life cycle, from raw material extraction through production, use, and end-of-life disposal.

- **ISO 14040:2006** — *Environmental management — Life cycle assessment — Principles and framework*. Defines the four phases of LCA, the methodological principles (relevance, scientific basis, transparency, comprehensiveness, etc.), and the high-level requirements.
- **ISO 14044:2006** — *Environmental management — Life cycle assessment — Requirements and guidelines*. Operationalises 14040 with detailed methodological requirements for each LCA phase, including system boundary setting, data quality, allocation, interpretation, and critical review.

The two are inseparable: 14040 sets the conceptual frame; 14044 specifies what must be done to conform to it. Together they are the methodological floor for all internationally recognised LCA work — including [ISO 14067 product carbon footprints](./14067-product-carbon-footprint.md), [BS EN 15978 embodied carbon assessments](../built-environment/bs-en-15978-embodied-carbon-buildings.md), Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) under ISO 14025, and most peer-reviewed LCA literature.

LCA in 14040/14044 is **multi-impact by design** — climate change is one of typically ten to fifteen impact categories covered in a full LCA, alongside acidification, eutrophication, ozone depletion, particulate matter, ecotoxicity, resource depletion, and others. For carbon-specific work, [ISO 14067](./14067-product-carbon-footprint.md) applies the 14040/14044 framework with a single-impact-category restriction (climate change).

## Why these standards matter for GreenCalculus

GreenCalculus is primarily a **carbon calculator platform**, not a full multi-impact LCA tool. But many of its product- and material-level emission factors are sourced from peer-reviewed and industry LCA work, which is itself 14040/14044-compliant. The standards matter in three direct ways:

1. **Upstream factor provenance.** Embodied-carbon factors used in construction calculators (concrete, steel, aluminium, timber, glass, insulation) are typically drawn from EPDs and LCA databases (Inventory of Carbon and Energy, ecoinvent, GaBi/Sphera, Idemat) — all of which are 14040/14044-aligned. Users tracing factor provenance need to know what that compliance means and what it does *not* guarantee.
2. **Product carbon footprints.** Where GreenCalculus produces a product-level result (e.g. a single-product carbon footprint for a manufactured good), the methodology is operationally a cradle-to-gate or cradle-to-grave LCA restricted to the climate-change impact category — consistent with [ISO 14067](./14067-product-carbon-footprint.md) and underlain by 14040/14044.
3. **Hot-spot identification.** Many corporate Scope 3 decisions (supplier selection, material substitution, end-of-life routing) are informed by LCA-style hot-spot analyses. GreenCalculus calculator outputs surface activity-level breakdowns that support this kind of analysis, even though they are not full multi-impact LCAs.

## The four phases of an LCA

ISO 14040 / 14044 define LCA as an iterative process with four phases:

| Phase | What it does | GreenCalculus relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Goal and scope definition** | Define the intended application, system function and functional unit, system boundaries, allocation procedures, impact categories, data quality requirements, type of critical review (if any) | Calculator goal-and-scope is product- or activity-level carbon; functional unit is typically one unit of product output or one unit of activity |
| **2. Life cycle inventory (LCI)** | Compile all inputs (materials, energy) and outputs (emissions, waste) for the system under study, normalised to the functional unit | Emission factors used by the calculator are LCI outputs from upstream LCA work |
| **3. Life cycle impact assessment (LCIA)** | Translate inventory flows into impact-category indicators (climate change, eutrophication, etc.) using characterisation factors | Climate-change LCIA uses IPCC GWPs as characterisation factors — [AR6 GWP-100](../ipcc/ar6-gwp-100.md) for GreenCalculus |
| **4. Interpretation** | Identify significant issues, evaluate completeness/sensitivity/consistency, draw conclusions, and recommend | Calculator output reporting includes activity-level disaggregation supporting basic interpretation |

The iterative nature is methodologically important: results from the inventory or impact-assessment phase may force a revision of goal and scope. A linear pass-through approach is not 14044-compliant.

## Functional unit — the LCA-specific concept users most often miss

The **functional unit** is the quantified function the system delivers — not the product itself. Two examples illustrate:

| Product | Naive unit (avoid) | Functional unit (correct) |
|---|---|---|
| Light bulb | One light bulb | 1,000 hours of illumination at 800 lumens |
| Disposable vs reusable cup | One cup | 1,000 servings of coffee over a defined service period |
| External wall (construction) | One m² of wall | One m² of wall with thermal performance U ≤ 0.18 W/m²K for 60 years |

The functional unit is the unit of comparison that makes the LCA result meaningful and comparable. For GreenCalculus product carbon calculators, the functional unit is always declared and is typically aligned with category Product Category Rules (PCRs) under ISO 14025 / EN 15804 where they exist.

## System boundary — and the cut-off problem

System boundary defines which life cycle stages are included:

| Boundary | What's included | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Cradle-to-gate** | Raw material extraction → manufactured product leaves factory gate | EPDs, supplier-facing PCFs |
| **Cradle-to-grave** | Full life cycle including use and end-of-life | Consumer-facing claims, regulatory compliance |
| **Cradle-to-cradle** | Cradle-to-grave with end-of-life recycled material credited back to the next system | Circular-economy claims (controversial; allocation-heavy) |
| **Gate-to-gate** | Single facility's contribution only | Internal hot-spot analysis (not a complete LCA) |

A 14044-compliant LCA must justify what is excluded (the "cut-off") and demonstrate that excluded items are not significant. A common 1% / 5% rule applies — items below the cut-off threshold may be omitted but cumulative excluded items must not exceed 5% of total system mass, energy, or environmental relevance.

## Allocation — the most contested 14044 procedure

When a single process produces multiple products (e.g. an oil refinery produces gasoline, diesel, naphtha, and bitumen from the same crude), the environmental burden of the process must be **allocated** to each co-product. ISO 14044 specifies a hierarchy:

1. **Avoid allocation** where possible — by subdividing the process or expanding the system to include the co-product's function.
2. **Allocate based on physical relationships** — mass, volume, energy content — that reflect the underlying causality.
3. **Allocate based on other relationships** — most often economic value — as a last resort.

This hierarchy matters because the same product can carry very different carbon footprints depending on the allocation choice. Construction-aggregate slag (a steel byproduct), recycled aluminium, biofuel co-products, and dairy/beef from the same herd are all canonical examples where allocation choice dominates the LCA result.

GreenCalculus users working with LCA-derived factors should be aware which allocation method the underlying factor used — typically declared in the EPD or LCI database entry — and consistent across comparisons.

## Attributional vs consequential LCA

A second major methodological choice, clarified in 14044 Amd 2 (2020):

| Approach | What it answers | GreenCalculus context |
|---|---|---|
| **Attributional LCA** | "What share of total environmental impact is attributable to this product, given the current state of the world?" | Default for product carbon footprints, EPDs, [Scope 3 Cat. 1](../ghg-protocol/scope-3-standard.md) factors |
| **Consequential LCA** | "What is the marginal environmental impact of a decision to produce one more (or one less) unit of this product?" | Used in scenario modelling, policy analysis, avoided-emissions accounting |

Most corporate inventory and product carbon work uses attributional LCA. Consequential approaches are appropriate for decision analysis (e.g. "should we switch our fleet to electric") but produce different — sometimes wildly different — results because they account for market substitution effects.

GreenCalculus calculators default to attributional results. Where consequential analysis is offered (e.g. fleet electrification scenarios), it is flagged explicitly.

## Critical review — the path to public comparative assertions

If an LCA is to support a **public comparative assertion** (e.g. "Product A has a lower carbon footprint than Product B"), ISO 14044 requires it to be reviewed by a panel of three independent experts. This is the most rigorous critical-review requirement in the standard.

Lower-stakes LCAs may use internal review or a single external reviewer, but the panel requirement for public comparative claims is widely interpreted as a regulatory floor — particularly under emerging EU rules (Green Claims Directive draft) that intersect with the EU's Empowering Consumers Directive (effective March 2026).

GreenCalculus calculator outputs are not in themselves critical-reviewed LCA studies. They are activity-level emissions calculations that can feed into a critically-reviewed LCA but do not discharge the review requirement.

## How GreenCalculus implements 14040/14044 alignment

GreenCalculus does not claim to *produce* 14044-conformant LCAs — that requires study-specific goal-and-scope definition, full multi-impact coverage, and (for public comparative claims) third-party critical review. What it does is provide **methodologically consistent emission factor data** that can be used within a 14040/14044-conformant study and produce activity-level outputs aligned with LCA conventions.

**LCI-aligned emission factors.** Embodied-carbon factors for construction materials, packaging, electronics, textiles, and other product categories come from sources that follow 14040/14044 (ICE v3.0, ecoinvent, GaBi-derived EPDs, peer-reviewed LCA literature). Each factor in the Master Brain carries provenance metadata: source database, vintage, system boundary, allocation method.

**Cradle-to-gate default with extensions.** Most embodied-carbon factors are cradle-to-gate. Where cradle-to-grave or use-phase factors are available, they are tagged separately so users can avoid double-counting.

**Functional unit declared.** Product-level calculators declare the functional unit explicitly (per tonne, per m², per unit, per kWh delivered, etc.) in line with LCA convention.

**AR6 GWP-100 characterisation.** Climate-change impact is calculated using [AR6 GWP-100](../ipcc/ar6-gwp-100.md) values throughout, consistent with the [GHG Protocol Corporate Standard](../ghg-protocol/corporate-standard.md) and current LCA practice.

**Provenance surfaced in audit trail.** Calculator outputs include factor source, vintage, system boundary, and any allocation method declared — supporting verification under [ISO 14064-1](./14064-1-organisation-ghg-quantification.md) or critical review under 14044.

## Important caveats

A few points worth flagging:

**1. LCA results are not comparable across studies without alignment.** Two LCAs of nominally the same product can produce very different results depending on goal-and-scope, functional unit, system boundary, allocation method, and database vintage. Critical review and PCR alignment are how the LCA community manages this.

**2. GreenCalculus is not a full LCA tool.** The calculators cover climate-change impact only. Users needing multi-impact assessment (water scarcity, eutrophication, land use, ecotoxicity) should use a dedicated LCA tool (openLCA, SimaPro, GaBi/Sphera, Brightway2) with full impact-method coverage.

**3. Database vintages decay.** LCI database emission factors are valid for the production technology, geographic scope, and energy mix of a specific year. Factors more than 5–10 years old may significantly under- or over-state current impacts (particularly for electricity-intensive products, where grid decarbonisation has been rapid). GreenCalculus tags every factor with its vintage and surfaces this in calculator outputs.

**4. The "carbon footprint" word is contested.** ISO 14067 reserves "product carbon footprint" for studies that meet specific 14040/14044-derived requirements. Casual or marketing use of the term should not be assumed to be 14067-compliant.

**5. Public comparative claims require critical review.** A GreenCalculus calculator showing that Product A has lower embodied carbon than Product B is not, on its own, a substantiated comparative claim under 14044 Section 7. Companies making such claims publicly must engage a critical review panel.
